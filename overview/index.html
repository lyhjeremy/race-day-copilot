Race day copilot
OCR, retrieval, a code verifier, and a LoRA-distilled Qwen2.5-3B

Guardrails and distillation, benchmarked

A pacing plan that has to pass six checks in code before you see it

Photograph a race course map and state a goal. Local OCR and Claude turn the photo into a course profile, retrieval adds pacing research from 14 knowledge cards, and Claude writes a km-by-km plan. verifier.py then checks the plan for coverage, split arithmetic, hill plausibility, fade, heat and citations, and any failure goes back to the model as a plain-English sentence. I also distilled the task into a 4-bit Qwen2.5-3B with LoRA, trained on a laptop from 1,321 verifier-passed teacher plans. On 70 held-out scenarios the student formats almost every plan correctly and then gets the arithmetic wrong.

The pipeline

Photo to verified plan

  1. Read the map. src/vision.py runs Tesseract OCR on the photo, then claude -p parses the text into a CourseProfile: distance, hill segments with trend and severity, and a confidence. A photo that isn't a course map gets a refusal.
  2. Retrieve. 14 knowledge cards on pacing research (negative-split rarity, hill effort, the heat tax, the wall) plus findings digests, embedded locally with sentence-transformers.
  3. Pack the prompt. A priority-based ContextBudgeter fits the course, the runner brief and the retrieved cards into a token budget and reports each section's share. Teacher and student get the same assembled prompt.
  4. Generate. Claude, or the local LoRA model, writes a structured PacingPlan: per-km target paces, predicted finish, fade allowance, heat adjustment, cautions, citations.
  5. Verify and retry. verify() returns a list of violation sentences. An empty list passes. Otherwise the sentences go into the next prompt, up to two retries.

The verifier

Six rules, with the tolerances in the code

01 Coverage
One split per km, plus the partial final km for distances like 21.1 or 42.2.
02 Sum
Splits add up to the goal within 90 seconds, or to the plan's own predicted finish if it flags the goal as unrealistic.
03 Plausibility
No split more than 12% slower than the mean pace, or 20% on a steep climb; on a downhill no split more than 20% faster.
04 Fade
Second half 0 to 8% slower than the first. A negative split is allowed only with a caution citing the 2.5% base rate. The plan's fade_allowance_pct must match its own splits within 0.5 points.
05 Heat
At 18°C or above, the per-km heat adjustment must fall between 0.75 and 4.5 seconds per degree over 15°C, a band derived from my Boston heat-tax finding of about 1 minute per °F.
06 Citations
At least one citation, and only ids that were retrieved for that prompt.

Earlier descriptions of this project counted five checks. The citation rule lives in the same function and is scored in the benchmark, so it is listed here as the sixth.

Base plans fail at parse or coverage, so the verifier returns early and the later checks never run on them; their 0.0% there means "not reached".

The LoRA student is close to the teacher on structure, coverage, hill plausibility, heat and citations; where it falls down is the two checks that need exact arithmetic across 42 numbers. A typical student plan misses the goal by 430 to 730 seconds, and its fade_allowance_pct often disagrees with its own splits in sign, for example reporting 4.5 while the splits compute −5.1%. In a blind, position-swapped LLM-judge comparison on 60 pairs, the teacher was preferred 93.3% of the time, the student never, with 6.7% ties.

The fine-tune

Four training attempts on a 16 GB Mac

Training data: 1,458 accepted scenarios across 6 course profiles, 14 goal times, 6 temperatures and 3 experience levels, split into 1,321 train, 67 validation and 70 test, with a leakage check. Training used mlx_lm LoRA on mlx-community/Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct-4bit, 16 layers, batch size 1, gradient accumulation 4, learning rate 5e-5, gradient checkpointing.

Sequence length
The planned 1,536 tokens was too short. The longest training example measured 4,197 tokens with the Qwen tokenizer, so the budget became 4,608, asserted before training.
Silent failure
Without gradient checkpointing, memory overflowed and training printed "Train loss 0.000, Trained Tokens 0" every report instead of crashing. A 10-iteration repro confirmed it; checkpointing brought peak memory to about 13 GB.
Divergence
Loss fell from 1.666 to 0.256 over 280 iterations, then climbed to 1.651 within three reports. Halving the learning rate and adding accumulation delayed it but didn't stop it.
Clipping
train_clipped.py reuses mlx_lm.lora's loop and adds clip_grad_norm, since the CLI has no clipping flag. It diverged again; gradient norms stayed at 0.18 to 0.35, under the 1.0 threshold, so clipping never engaged.
Decision
Ship the best checkpoint, about 400 clean iterations at validation loss 0.226, and leave the next hypothesis (a constant learning rate with no decay) for later.

Limitations

What this doesn't show

The student model is not usable on its own yet: no held-out plan passed every check. It would need either a decaying learning-rate schedule and more training, or the arithmetic moved out of the model, for example by having it emit relative effort and computing the splits in code.

The 70 test scenarios come from the same six synthetic course profiles as the training data, so the benchmark doesn't test unseen course shapes or photographed maps. The LLM judge is Claude, which is also the teacher. And the archived raw outputs in eval/raw_outputs_*.json were truncated at 4,000 characters when saved; scoring ran on the full text before truncation, so the metrics stand, but the archive is incomplete.

Plans are pacing guidance for adult runners, not medical advice. The app runs on your own machine; there is no hosted demo.
