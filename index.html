Pacing plans that are checked before you see them

Photograph a race course map and state a goal time. Local OCR and Claude read the map into a course profile, retrieval adds pacing research, and Claude writes a km-by-km plan. Six checks in plain Python (coverage, split sum, hill plausibility, fade, heat, citations) must pass before the plan is shown; failures go back to the model as sentences. A LoRA fine-tune of Qwen2.5-3B, trained on a laptop, tries to do the same job locally.

Fine-tuning taught the student the format: parse success went from 55.7% to 95.7%, and it fails coverage, plausibility and heat on under 5% of plans. It fails on arithmetic. 82.9% of its plans miss the goal by more than 90 seconds, and 91.4% report a fade that doesn't match their own splits. Split by scenario id, zero-shot, no retries, same prompt for all three.

The six checks

  • CoverageOne split per km, plus the partial last km.
  • SumSplits total the goal within 90 seconds.
  • PlausibilityEach split within its segment's allowance.
  • Fade0 to 8% slower in the second half, or a flagged negative split citing the 2.5% base rate.
  • HeatAt 18°C and up, an adjustment band derived from my Boston heat-tax finding.
  • CitationsOnly ids that were actually retrieved.

Training took four attempts

1,458 verifier-passed teacher plans (96.0% of 1,519 attempts) trained an MLX LoRA adapter on a 16 GB Mac. Along the way: a sequence length 2.7 times too short, a memory overflow that printed "loss 0.000" instead of crashing, and three divergences, the last despite a custom gradient-clipping loop. The published adapter is the best checkpoint, about 400 clean iterations at validation loss 0.226.
